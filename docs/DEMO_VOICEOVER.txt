Children already turn simple marks into stories. WallAlive asks: what if those characters could step off the wall and into the room?

A child explicitly opens the camera and approves one drawing. Local math isolates the centered ink, closes small gaps, flood-fills the silhouette, and traces its boundary. An interior distance field inflates wide regions more than narrow ones. Three.js Marching Cubes turns that 64-cubed field into one closed rounded mesh with a curved artwork front, real sides, and a generated back. Watch the full turn: this is volume, not a thick cutout. Nothing is uploaded, and no eyes or limbs are invented.

Eight strict WebMCP tools let a browser agent inspect approved shape and color, reconstruct the volume, create a personality, place the character, animate it, and perform a short story. Human and agent actions change the same visible world, with every author shown in history.

The privacy boundary is the point: the camera is not a tool. An agent cannot open it, capture a frame, or upload a child’s room. The child chooses what enters the shared imagination.

Supported devices get WebXR surface placement. Everywhere else, the camera overlay preserves the full play loop.

WallAlive. Draw it. Wake it. Play.
